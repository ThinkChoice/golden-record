Week 5 football notebook.

Developing observations about the games and players that catch my attention this week. Notes will grow as the week unfolds.

Opening notes

This week's games offer questions about quarterback availability, defensive pressure, running-game identity, and how teams adapt when key players are limited.

Week 5 matchups

A closer look at the strengths we trust, the weaknesses that invite doubt, and the matchups that make this week interesting.

Buccaneers at Cowboys

Dallas brought a convincing collection of offensive strengths into Thursday: Dak Prescott, CeeDee Lamb and a running game capable of sharing the burden. Tampa Bay offered a different possibility. Could its defense and Bucky Irving's running game make the afternoon small enough that quarterback uncertainty wouldn't decide it? That was the tension that interested us before kickoff.

Eagles at Jaguars

Jacksonville's defense has earned genuine trust, and its running game has become another reliable source of offense. Philadelphia's familiar talent is still recognizable, but injuries have disrupted the structure that ordinarily supports it. Can the Eagles rediscover that identity in London, or will the Jaguars' pressure and balance set the terms?

Raiders at Patriots

Brock Bowers, Ashton Jeanty and a disruptive defense make Las Vegas one of the more intriguing collections of talent this week. But New England's recent offensive improvement suggests the Patriots might be changing faster than older performances reveal. Can Maye's developing attack keep the Raiders from making this a game about Bowers, Jeanty and defensive disruption?

Texans at Titans

Houston's offensive foundation meets a Tennessee team looking for ways to disrupt routine possessions. Which team establishes the tempo?

Browns at Jets

Breece Hall's availability and workload shape the Jets' possibilities. Garrett Wilson offers explosiveness, while Cleveland has an opportunity to challenge the supporting structure.

Colts at Steelers

Jonathan Taylor is the obvious force for Indianapolis, but Pittsburgh can answer with Jaylen Warren and one of the stronger defensive units we've studied. The Steelers appear to have more than one way to shape the game; the Colts may need Taylor to do an extraordinary amount of work. Can he?

Giants at Commanders

Cam Skattebo and New York's defense give the Giants a real identity. Washington's identity could change almost overnight if Jayden Daniels returns with his usual mobility and command. This is a matchup where one quarterback's availability may matter more than the rest of the comparison.

Bengals at Dolphins

Cincinnati's receiving talent is the main attraction, subject to availability. Miami will try to keep that passing rhythm from settling in.

Vikings at Saints

Minnesota's defense and special teams have been remarkably dependable, and Aaron Jones gives the offense a way to control possessions. New Orleans can answer with the Shough–Olave connection. If the Saints force Minnesota into a passing contest, will the Vikings have enough offense to support their defensive strength?

Bears at Packers

Chicago's running backs have given the Bears a surprisingly sturdy foundation. Green Bay's receiving options, especially Watson and Golden, can threaten a defense quickly, but the Packers' overall offensive consistency remains a question. Can Chicago keep the game centered on its ground attack while adjusting to a different quarterback?

Broncos at Chargers

Denver's defensive secondary and the Chargers' protection questions create a compelling contest of strengths and vulnerabilities.

Lions at Cardinals

Detroit's offense is easy to admire: Gibbs, Goff, St. Brown and LaPorta can all change a game. But the Lions' defense has been much harder to trust. Arizona's opportunity is to turn that weakness into a real contest, provided its own offensive-line problems don't prevent it.

49ers at Seahawks

Purdy, McCaffrey and Kittle make San Francisco formidable across several positions. Seattle counters with Jaxon Smith-Njigba and a defense capable of making even a strong passing attack uncomfortable. Could Seattle's defensive organization protect its own less convincing quarterback play, or will San Francisco's broader offense eventually solve it?

Ravens at Falcons

Bijan Robinson and Drake London give Atlanta meaningful offensive strengths, while Derrick Henry and Zay Flowers keep Baltimore dangerous even amid quarterback uncertainty. But Lamar Jackson's availability changes almost everything. What kind of Ravens team will Atlanta actually face?

Bills at Rams

Josh Allen and James Cook give Buffalo considerable offensive power. Los Angeles answers with Kyren Williams, Puka Nacua and Davante Adams, a group that can attack in several ways. Buffalo's recent turnover problems make this especially interesting: can the Rams keep their offense organized long enough to force the Bills into uncomfortable decisions?
