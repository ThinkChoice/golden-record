Football · Week 3

NFL notes for the week.

Seven games that gave me something interesting to think about—mostly questions about game shape, pressure, quarterback burden, injuries and the small matchups that can change a Sunday.

Jets at Lions

Detroit looks built to score its way out of trouble. The Jets' best answer may be Breece Hall.

Hall gives New York a way to change the pace of the game without asking Geno Smith to win a shootout. If Hall can keep drives alive, eat clock, create first downs and force Detroit to spend more of the afternoon watching from the sideline, that limits how often the Lions get to lean on the part of their team we trust most—the offense.

New York's defense adds another layer. The Jets have already shown enough through two games to suggest they can make an opponent work. I don't trust that defense as much as I trust the Hall-centered game plan, but it gives New York another way to slow Detroit down and keep the game within reach.

And then there is Garrett Wilson. He doesn't necessarily help New York control the game in the same way Hall can, but he gives the Jets some explosive scoring ability of their own. We've seen both Hall and Wilson take over stretches of games during their young college and NFL careers. If Hall can help dictate the pace and the defense can create enough resistance, Wilson is the player who could suddenly turn a manageable game into a dangerous one for Detroit.

So, can the Jets win this game? I think they can. Will they? I'm a lot less sure about that. And if they do, what actually gets them there? Is it Hall controlling the pace? Is it the defense giving Detroit just enough trouble? Is it Wilson supplying the explosion? Is it some combination of all three? Or does Geno Smith pick this particular Sunday to make all of this analysis look unnecessarily complicated? Surely not Geno, right?

Chargers at Bills

Buffalo has spent two weeks looking like a team that can simply score its way through problems. That's impressive, but it also makes me wonder what happens when the offense doesn't completely control the afternoon.

The Chargers have given us very little reason to trust them as a team so far. They're 0–2. Justin Herbert has been erratic. The defense hasn't looked especially convincing. If this were just a question of which team has played better football, Buffalo would be the easy answer.

But there are enough pieces in Los Angeles that I don't quite want to dismiss them.

Omarion Hampton is one of them. His second game was much more substantial than his first: our internal scoring system gave him a 130.5 performance, driven by 94 rushing yards, a touchdown, useful receiving work and repeated first-down production. That's the kind of running-back game that can help keep an explosive opponent off the field and make the afternoon less comfortable for Buffalo.

Ladd McConkey is another. We've already seen the explosive version of him this season, and he remains one of the Chargers players I trust most to produce something meaningful if Herbert gives him enough opportunities.

Then there is Herbert himself, who I don't especially trust right now—but volatility cuts both ways. He's been bad enough to lose you a game and talented enough to suddenly make a difficult matchup look much less orderly than expected.

Even the Chargers defense has shown a little life. It hasn't been good overall, but it has produced sacks and a turnover. Maybe that means nothing against Josh Allen. Or maybe one or two disruptive possessions are all Los Angeles needs if Hampton can shorten the game and McConkey can create something on the other side.

And that is probably the real source of my interest: Buffalo looks trustworthy because of its offense, not because it has been controlling games defensively. The Chargers look untrustworthy almost everywhere—but they have a coach I still believe in, a running back coming off a strong performance, a receiver I trust, and a quarterback volatile enough to make the game strange.

So what happens if Hampton gives Los Angeles real control of the clock? What if McConkey gets loose a few times? What if the Chargers defense finds two or three useful stops instead of needing to dominate? And what if Herbert is the good version of Herbert for one afternoon?

Buffalo should still be the team I trust more.

But is Los Angeles unstable enough—in the useful sense this time—to catch a Buffalo team that has already shown it can be dragged into high-scoring, uncomfortable games?

Raiders at Saints

Las Vegas' defensive front gives this game its most interesting shape. If the Raiders can bother New Orleans up front, the rest of the matchup may look very different than it does on paper.

Player/story to watch: Brock Bowers' availability and role.

Patriots at Jaguars

This one interests me as a test of which team can make the opposing quarterback uncomfortable first. New England's defensive structure is the piece I trust most in the matchup; Jacksonville's answer is what I want to see.

Question: which quarterback is forced to carry more of the afternoon?

Ravens at Cowboys

This feels less orderly than the team names suggest. Injuries and changing pieces on both sides make me curious whether either team can establish a stable identity early—or whether the game stays messy and quarterback-driven.

Stories to watch: Zay Flowers, Baltimore's line and Dallas' defensive health.

Rams at Broncos

The Rams have a lot of ways to make a game comfortable for Matthew Stafford. Denver's defense and coaching can do the same for Bo Nix. The interesting question is which structure survives once one quarterback is asked to do more.

Question: can Denver keep Nix from having to match Stafford throw for throw?

Vikings at Buccaneers

Minnesota wants pressure to matter. Tampa Bay has shown it can answer pressure. Minnesota also wants its run game to matter; Tampa Bay wants to take that away. It's a nice strength-against-strength game.

Question: which team's strength actually changes the other's preferred game?

Why these seven?

No grand ranking. They're simply the games where something in the matchup made me want to look twice. Sometimes that means a player, sometimes a pressure matchup, sometimes an injury, and sometimes a game that just looks unusually difficult to reduce to one clean story.

After Sunday: the fun part is coming back to these questions and seeing which story actually happened.
