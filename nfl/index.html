Football · Week 3

NFL notes for the week.

Seven games that gave me something interesting to think about—mostly questions about game shape, pressure, quarterback burden, injuries and the small matchups that can change a Sunday.

Jets at Lions

Detroit has the cleaner overall profile, and Jared Goff is the quarterback I trust more to run the kind of game his team wants. That is exactly why the Jets are interesting to me: their best story is probably not Geno Smith trying to match Detroit throw for throw. It is New York finding enough control elsewhere to keep the afternoon from turning into that kind of contest.

Breece Hall is the player I keep coming back to. The retained early-season research gives him 38 carries for 131 yards plus seven catches for 79 yards, and the reconstructed Week 1 line included a 102-yard rushing game. If Hall can give New York sustained offense, first downs and possessions that do not depend on Geno creating everything, he is the clearest route to making Detroit play a less comfortable game.

Garrett Wilson is the more explosive alternative I would prefer to see become the answer. The retained research has 11 catches for 136 yards and a touchdown on 14 targets. If Wilson is the Jet who really changes the matchup, that probably means New York has found chunk plays and receiving production without asking Geno to manufacture an entire shootout by himself.

So my question is more specific than whether the Jets can “hang around”: if New York seriously challenges Detroit, is Hall the player who controls enough of the game to make that possible, or does Wilson become the bigger playmaker who changes its shape? I am much less interested in a version where the answer has to be Geno Smith carrying them.

Players I'm watching: Breece Hall first; Garrett Wilson as the preferred explosive answer. Game question: can either give the Jets enough offense to keep Detroit from dictating a scoring race?

Chargers at Bills

Buffalo's pressure against Justin Herbert is the part I keep coming back to. Herbert's quickest answer may be getting the ball to Ladd McConkey before the pressure can define the play.

Question: does Buffalo's pressure win, or create opportunities behind it?

Raiders at Saints

Las Vegas' defensive front gives this game its most interesting shape. If the Raiders can bother New Orleans up front, the rest of the matchup may look very different than it does on paper.

Player/story to watch: Brock Bowers' availability and role.

Patriots at Jaguars

This one interests me as a test of which team can make the opposing quarterback uncomfortable first. New England's defensive structure is the piece I trust most in the matchup; Jacksonville's answer is what I want to see.

Question: which quarterback is forced to carry more of the afternoon?

Ravens at Cowboys

This feels less orderly than the team names suggest. Injuries and changing pieces on both sides make me curious whether either team can establish a stable identity early—or whether the game stays messy and quarterback-driven.

Stories to watch: Zay Flowers, Baltimore's line and Dallas' defensive health.

Rams at Broncos

The Rams have a lot of ways to make a game comfortable for Matthew Stafford. Denver's defense and coaching can do the same for Bo Nix. The interesting question is which structure survives once one quarterback is asked to do more.

Question: can Denver keep Nix from having to match Stafford throw for throw?

Vikings at Buccaneers

Minnesota wants pressure to matter. Tampa Bay has shown it can answer pressure. Minnesota also wants its run game to matter; Tampa Bay wants to take that away. It's a nice strength-against-strength game.

Question: which team's strength actually changes the other's preferred game?

Why these seven?

No grand ranking. They're simply the games where something in the matchup made me want to look twice. Sometimes that means a player, sometimes a pressure matchup, sometimes an injury, and sometimes a game that just looks unusually difficult to reduce to one clean story.

After Sunday: the fun part is coming back to these questions and seeing which story actually happened.
