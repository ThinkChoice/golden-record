Two games, two different kinds of trust.

Our opening Week 6 football diagnoses are in: Seattle's concentrated advantage against Denver, and Jacksonville's stronger collective structure against a Houston team with a new reason to believe. The rest of the slate is still under review.

Thursday night: Seahawks at Broncos

Prognosis · Seattle

Seattle Trust: 33⅓¢ · Denver Doubt: 10¢

Jaxon Smith-Njigba is the first thing that stands out. His season-long production in our custom scoring system dwarfs the opposing receivers, and Seattle also brings the stronger defensive and coaching signals. Denver's secondary has taken serious hits, with Patrick Surtain II and Talanoa Hufanga unavailable. That makes the JSN matchup especially difficult to ignore.

Denver still has a route through Bo Nix and its running game. A breakout night from Nix could change the script, but we don't see enough supporting evidence to pay a generous price for the upset. Seattle is the stronger organism; Denver's counterargument is more speculative.

The listed cents are our pregame maximum acquisition thresholds, not current Robinhood market quotes, purchase confirmations, or guaranteed returns.

London: Texans at Jaguars

Prognosis · Jacksonville

Jacksonville Trust: 33⅓¢ · Houston Doubt: 18¢

Jacksonville has the better collective shape. Its head-coaching and team-defense scores are stronger, and Bhayshul Tuten gives the Jaguars the more convincing running-back Trust. Jacksonville's familiarity with London adds a modest contextual advantage.

But Houston is a more interesting underdog than its record might suggest. C.J. Stroud's current scoring is ahead of Trevor Lawrence's, Nico Collins is a major receiving force, and the arrival of Marvin Harrison Jr. creates an intriguing new possibility. A new receiver doesn't automatically become a proven contributor in his first game, but a familiar connection with Stroud could give Houston's offense a shot of adrenaline.

There is another point of vulnerability: Jacksonville's injured offensive line against Houston's pass rush. That combination makes this a live divisional matchup rather than a simple dismissal of the Texans. We still trust Jacksonville to win, while respecting Houston enough to consider its upset only at a sufficiently discounted price.

Harrison's immediate role and both teams' defensive availability remain pregame questions. The thresholds above express our own pricing discipline, not the sportsbook's forecast.
